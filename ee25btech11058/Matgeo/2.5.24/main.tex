\textbf{Question:}\par
If $|\vec{a}| = 2$, $|\vec{b}| = 7$ and $\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = 3\hat{\imath} + 2\hat{\jmath} + 6\hat{k}$, find the angle between $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$. \par
\textbf{Solution:}\par

Let the vector $\vec{c} = \vec{a} \times \vec{b}$. We are given:
\begin{align}
    \norm{\vec{a}} &= 2 \\
    \norm{\vec{b}} &= 7 \\
    \vec{c} &= \myvec{3 \\ 2 \\ 6}
\end{align}
The squared magnitude of a vector $\vec{v}$ can be found using the matrix product $\vec{v}^\top\vec{v}$.
We calculate the squared magnitude of $\vec{c}$:
\begin{align}
    \norm{\vec{c}}^2 &= \vec{c}^\top\vec{c} \\
    &= \myvec{3 & 2 & 6} \myvec{3 \\ 2 \\ 6} \\
    &= (3)(3) + (2)(2) + (6)(6) \\
    &= 9 + 4 + 36 \\
    &= 49
\end{align}
Therefore, the magnitude of $\vec{c}$ is:
\begin{align}
    \norm{\vec{c}} = \sqrt{49} = 7
\end{align}
The magnitude of the cross product is also defined by the relation:
\begin{align}
    \norm{\vec{c}} = \norm{\vec{a} \times \vec{b}} = \norm{\vec{a}}\norm{\vec{b}}\sin(\theta)
\end{align}
where $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$. Substituting the known values:
\begin{align}
    7 &= (2)(7)\sin(\theta) \\
    7 &= 14\sin(\theta)
\end{align}
Solving for $\sin(\theta)$:
\begin{align}
    \sin(\theta) = \frac{7}{14} = \frac{1}{2}
\end{align}
This implies that the angle $\theta$ is:
\begin{align}
    \theta = \frac{\pi}{6} \text{ or } 30\degree
\end{align}
